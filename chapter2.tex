\chapter{Chordal \texorpdfstring{$(P_5,\text{house})$}{(P5,house)}-free graphs}
\label{cap:chordal}

In the hope to ease the introduction of the techniques we use throughout the
chapters, we start our study by looking at chordal graphs, which tend to be
well-behaved when one is working with partitions. Since we consider the chordal
graphs which are $(P_5,\text{house})$-free, asking the graph to be
$\text{house}$-free is redundant once we know it is chordal. Thus, we will not
mention the abscense of the house in any of the results for the present chapter.

Before we get into the problem, we should note that the case about to be
analyzed has already been solved in \cite{chordal}. In there, the authors
present a polynomial-time algorithm that decides if a chordal graph (not
necessarily $P_5$-free) is polar. Such an algorithm depends on a linear-time
subroutine that decides monopolarity, and finds a monopolar partition if one
exists. Nonetheless, while authors mention that the subroutine could be modified
so that it outputs a no-certificate as a proof of non-membership if the graph is
not monopolar, they do not specify if this additional procedure can be done in
linear time nor whether additional data structures are needed. Thereby, we
commit to develop a full linear-time certyfing algorithm for chordal $P_5$-free
graphs.

\section{Characterization of monopolarity}
\label{sec:chordal_characterization}

We begin with the following auxiliary lemma that will help us prove that some
graphs are indeed monopolar obstructions.

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lem:monopolar_diamond}
    Let $G$ be a graph with an induced copy of the diamond. Then, any monopolar
    partition $(A,B)$ of $G$ must be such that the chord of the diamond is in
    $B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Let $\p{a,b,c,d,a}$ be an induced copy of $C_4$ in $G$ such that $a$ is
    adjacent to $c$ and $b$ is not adjacent to $d$. Suppose $G$ is monopolar and
    $(A,B)$ is a monopolar partition of $G$. If $a\in A$, it follows from $A$
    being stable that $\p{b,c,d}$ is an induced copy of $P_3$ in $B$, which
    contradicts that $G[B]$ is a cluster. Thus, $a\in B$, and similarly follows
    that $c\in B$.

\end{proof}

Up next we show the list of minimal obstructions for monopolarity in chordal
$P_5$-free graphs. Do notice that in \cite{juraj}, Stacho proposes a grammar
that generates all minimal monopolar obstructions for chordal graphs, with the
caveat that there is an infinite number of them. By using the rules of the
grammar, one could exhaustively prove that the graphs in
\Cref{fig:chordal-p5-free-obstructions} are the only $P_5$-free graphs derived.
However, for completeness, we proceed to show that these indeed are minimal
obstructions. Then, we will use them not only to deduce that this is the
complete list, but to extract the linear-time certyfing algorithm we are looking
for.

Having identified the minimal obstructions, we devote ourselves to proving that
these are all. For that purpose we turn to split graphs which are those whose
vertex set can be partitioned into a stable set and a clique. In particular,
split graphs are monopolar. Since split graphs are exactly the $\l{2K_2, C_4,
C_5}$-free graphs, it follows that a chordal graph is split if and only if it is
$2K_2$-free. In \cite{heggernesNJC14,hellDAM141}, linear-time certifying
algorithms for recognizing split graphs are shown. Thus, given a chordal graph,
one can run such an algorithm to decide whether the graph is split, which in
turn implies the graph is monopolar, or get a $2K_2$ as a certificate of
non-membership. Therefore, to solve \textsc{Monopolarity} in chordal graphs, it
suffices to solve it for those graphs that contain an induced copy of $2K_2$. 

Throughout the rest of this section, we assume that the graphs have an induced
copy of $2K_2$, whose vertex set is $Z=\l{a,b,c,d}$ and it is such that $ab$ and
$cd$ are the disjoint edges of the $2K_2$. The following lemma asserts there are
two types of $P_5$-free graphs with an induced copy of $2K_2$.

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lem:chordal_connectedness}
    Let $G$ be a connected $P_5$-free graph with an induced copy of a $2K_2$
    having vertex set $Z$. One of the following must be true:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
        \item There is a vertex adjacent to both edges of $G[Z]$.
        \item There are adjacent vertices $x$ and $y$ with $x$ complete to the
        edge $ab$ and anticomplete to the edge $cd$, whereas $y$ is complete to
        $cd$ and anticomplete to $ab$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Suppose (i) does not happen. Since $G$ is connected, there exists a path of
    minimum length, $P$, whose initial vertex is in $\l{a,b}$, its final vertex
    is in $\l{c,d}$, and all middle vertices are not in $Z$. Since (i) is not
    true, such a path has at least 4 vertices. Moreover, if it has at least $5$
    vertices, it follows that $P$ contains a copy of $P_5$, which is induced
    since $P$ is of minimum length.

    Therefore, suppose without loss of generality that there are adjacent
    vertices $x$ and $y$ such that $P=\p{a,x,y,c}$. Since $P$ is of minimum
    length, $x$ is anticomplete to $\l{c,d}$ and $y$ is anticomplete to
    $\l{a,b}$. Moreover, if $x$ is not adjacent to $b$, notice $\p{b,a,x,y,c}$
    is an induced copy of $P_5$ (see \Cref{fig:chordal_connectedness}).

        \centering

            on grid, edge]
            \node[Label] (z) {};

\end{proof}

The next lemmas describe the structure of the exact neighborhoods of subsets of
$Z$ given that a graph is free of the minimal obstructions shown in
\Cref{fig:chordal-p5-free-obstructions}. For simplicity throughout the chapter,
exact neighborhoods $N_X$ when $X$ is of size one will be called 1-bags.
Similarly, sets $N_X$ when $X$ is of size two and three will be called 2-bags
and 3-bags, respectively.

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lem:neighborhoods_chordal_anti}
    Let $G$ be a connected chordal $P_5$-free graph with and induced copy of
    $2K_2$ having vertex set $Z$ and free of the graphs depicted in

    subsets of $Z$.

        \item If $N_X$ and $N_Y$ are 1-bags complete to vertices within an edge
            of $G[Z]$, then $N_X$ is anticomplete to $N_Y$.

        \item If $N_X$ is a 1-bag, then $N_X$ is anticomplete to $N_Z$.
            \label{lem:chordal_anti_1}

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}

The upcoming lemma deals with the structure within exact neighborhoods.

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lem:neighborhoods_chordal_types}
    Let $G$ be a connected chordal $P_5$-free graph with and induced copy of
    $2K_2$ having vertex set $Z$ and free of the graphs depicted in
    \Cref{fig:chordal-p5-free-obstructions}. Let $X$ and $Y$ be different
    subsets of $Z$.
    \begin{lemenum}
        \item If $N_X$ is a 2-bag anticomplete to no edge of $G[Z]$, then $N_X$
            is empty. \label{lem:chordal_types_2}
        \item If $N_X$ is a bag complete to vertices in both edges of $G[Z]$,
            then $N_X$ is a clique. \label{lem:chordal_types_clique_bag}
        \item The exact neighborhood $N_Z$ has cardinality at most one.
            \label{lem:chordal_types_1}
        \item If $N_X$ is a 2-bag complete to an edge of $G[Z]$, then $G[N_X]$
            is split. \label{lem:chordal_types_6}
        \item At most one bag is complete to vertices in both edges of $G[Z]$.
            \label{lem:chordal_types_3}
        \item If $N_X$ is a 3-bag and $N_Y$ is the complementary 1-bag, then at
            least one of $N_X$ and $N_Y$ is empty.
            \label{lem:chordal_types_pending}
    \end{lemenum}
\end{lemma}

As we will see, the proof of the main theorem requires to check the case when
$N_Z$ is nonempty. In order to facilitate its proof, the next three lemmas
provide additional relations between the exact neighborhoods given that $N_Z$ is
nonempty. If that is the case, notice that, by \Cref{lem:chordal_types_1}, there
is just one vertex in $N_Z$, namely $z$. This first result state relations of
anticompleteness.

\begin{lemma}
    Let $G$ be a connected chordal $P_5$-free graph with and induced copy of
    $2K_2$ having vertex set $Z$ and free of the graphs depicted in
    \Cref{fig:chordal-p5-free-obstructions}. Let $X$ and $Y$ be different
    nonempty proper subsets of $Z$. Suppose that $N_Z=\l{z}$.
    \begin{lemenum}
        \item If $N_X$ and $N_Y$ are bags anticomplete to different edges
            of $G[Z]$, then $N_X$ is anticomplete to $N_Y$.
            \label{lem:chordal_anti_3}
        \item If $N_X$ is a bag anticomplete to an edge of $G[Z]$, then
            $N_\varnothing$ is anticomplete to $N_X \setminus N(z)$. In
            particular, 1-bags are anticomplete to $N_\varnothing$.
            \label{lem:chordal_anti_5}
        \item Let $C$ be a connected component of $G[N_\varnothing]$ not
            anticomplete to $z$. If $N_X$ is a 2-bag complete to an edge of
            $G[Z]$, then $N_X$ is anticomplete to $C$.
            \label{lem:chordal_anti_2_bag}
        \item If $N_X$ is a 2-bag complete to an edge of $G[Z]$, and $N_Y$ is a
            1-bag, then $N_Y$ is anticomplete to $N_X \setminus N(z)$.
            \label{lem:chordal_anti_2_bag_2}

\end{lemma}

            $C_4$ or $H_2$, depending on whether $x$ and $y$ are adjacent to $z$
            or not (see \Cref{fig:chordal_anti_3}). Notice this argument also
            applies if $N_X$ and/or $N_Y$ are 1-bags. We conclude that $N_X$ is
            anticomplete to $N_Y$.

Now we give a lemma for structure within bags.

\begin{lemma}
    Let $G$ be a connected chordal $P_5$-free graph with and induced copy of
    $2K_2$ having vertex set $Z$ and free of the graphs depicted in
    \Cref{fig:chordal-p5-free-obstructions}. Let $X$ and $Y$ be different proper
    subsets of $Z$. Suppose that $N_Z=\l{z}$.
    \begin{lemenum}
        \item If $N_X$ and $N_Y$ are 1-bags complete to vertices in different
            edges of $G[Z]$, then at least one of $N_X$ and $N_Y$ is empty.
            \label{lem:chordal_types_5}
        \item If $N_X$ is a 2-bag complete to an edge of $G[Z]$, then $N_X \cap
            N(z)$ is a clique. \label{lem:chordal_types_K_clique}
        \item The set $G[N_\varnothing \cap N(z)]$ is a cluster.
            \label{lem:chordal_types_K_cluster}
        \item If $N_X$ is a 1-bag, then $N_X$ is stable.
            \label{lem:chordal_types_4}
        \item If $N_X$ is a 2-bag complete to an edge of $G[Z]$, then
            non-isolated vertices of $G[N_\varnothing \setminus N(z)]$ are
            anticomplete to $N_X \cap N(z)$.
            \label{lem:chordal_types_trivial}
        \item If $N_X$ is a 2-bag complete to an edge of $G[Z]$ and $N_Y$ is a
            1-bag complete to a vertex in the other edge of $G[Z]$, then at
            least one of $N_{X} \setminus N(z)$ and $N_Y$ is empty.
            \label{lem:chordal_types_empty}
    \end{lemenum}
\end{lemma}

Now we are ready to prove the main theorem.

\begin{theorem}
    \label{thm:monopolar_chordal}
    A connected chordal $P_5$-free graph $G$ with an induced copy of $2K_2$
    having vertex set $Z$ is monopolar if and only if it does not contain any of
    the graphs depicted in \Cref{fig:chordal-p5-free-obstructions} as an induced
    subgraph.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Since monopolarity is hereditary, it follows from
    \Cref{prop:minimal_obs_chordal} that if $G$ is monopolar, then it does not
    contain any of the mentioned graphs as an induced subgraph.

    Suppose that $G$ does not contain any of the graphs depicted in
    \Cref{fig:chordal-p5-free-obstructions}. We proceed by considering the
    following cases:

    \paragraph{Case 1: $N_Z$ is nonempty.} By \Cref{lem:chordal_types_1}, $N_Z$
    has exactly one vertex, namely $z$. Additionally, \Cref{lem:chordal_types_3}
    implies that any other bag is anticomplete to at least one edge of $G[Z]$.
    Also, by \Cref{lem:chordal_types_5}, suppose without loss of generality that
    both $N_c$ and $N_d$ are empty.

    Let $K_{ab}$ be the set of vertices of $N_{ab}$ that are complete to $z$. By
    \Cref{lem:chordal_types_K_clique}, $K_{ab}$ is a clique. Similarly, the set
    of vertices in $N_{cd}$ that are complete to $z$, $K_{cd}$, is a clique.
    Furthermore, $K_{ab}$ and $K_{cd}$ are anticomplete to each other by
    \Cref{lem:chordal_anti_3}. Then,
    \[
        K_{ab}\cup K_{cd} \cup Z
    \]
    induces a $2$-cluster. Now let $\mathcal{C}$ be the set of connected
    components in $G[N_\varnothing]$. In particular, let $\mathcal{C}_z$ be the
    set of connected components in $\mathcal{C}$ that are not anticomplete to
    $z$. For each $C\in \mathcal{C}_z$, let $C_z$ be the graph induced by the
    vertices of $C$ that are adjacent to $z$. Notice
    \Cref{lem:chordal_types_K_cluster} implies that $C_z$ is a cluster. Also,
    \Cref{lem:chordal_anti_2_bag} ensures that $C_z$ is anticomplete to $K_{ab}$
    and $K_{cd}$, so
    \[
        K_{ab} \cup K_{cd} \bigcup_{C\in \mathcal{C}_z} V_{C_z} \cup Z
    \]
    induces a cluster.
    
    Meanwhile, because of \Cref{lem:chordal_types_4}, we have that $N_a$ and
    $N_b$ are stable, while \Cref{lem:chordal_anti_2} implies that they are
    anticomplete to each other. Moreover, \Cref{lem:chordal_anti_1} ensures that
    both $N_a$ and $N_b$ are anticomplete to $z$, so
    \[
        N_a \cup N_b \cup \l{z}
    \]
    is stable. Observe that each component $C\in \mathcal{C}\setminus
    \mathcal{C}_z$ is anticomplete to $z$. Additionally,
    \Cref{lem:chordal_anti_5} implies that $V_C$ is anticomplete to both $N_a$
    and $N_b$.  Since $G$ is connected, $V_C$ must be not anticomplete to
    $N_{ab} \cup N_{cd}$. Due to \Cref{lem:chordal_anti_5}, $V_C$ can only be
    not anticomplete to either $K_{ab}$ or $K_{cd}$. Nonetheless,
    \Cref{lem:chordal_types_trivial} implies that $C$ is the trivial graph, so
    \[
        N_a \cup N_b \cup \bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}\setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C 
        \cup \l{z}
    \]
    is stable. We still have to decide how to arrange the following sets:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $N_{ab}\setminus K_{ab}$,
        \item $N_{cd}\setminus K_{cd}$, and
        \item sets $V_{C-C_z}$ for $C \in \mathcal{C}_z$.
    \end{itemize}
    We proceed by taking subcases:

    \paragraph{Case 1.1: $N_{ab}$ is not complete to $z$.} By
    \Cref{lem:chordal_comp_1}, $N_{cd}$ is complete to $z$, so in fact, $N_{cd}
    = K_{cd}$. 
    
    We claim that, for each $C\in \mathcal{C}_z$, $V_{C-C_z}$ is empty. Indeed,
    since $N_{ab}\setminus K_{ab}$ is nonempty, let $x\in N_{ab}\setminus
    K_{ab}$. If $V_{C - C_z}$ is nonempty, since $C$ is connected, there are
    adjacent vertices $w\in V_{C - C_z}$ and $w'\in V_{C_z}$. By
    \Cref{lem:chordal_anti_5}, $w$ and $w'$ are anticomplete to $x$, so
    $\p{w,w',z,a,x}$ is an induced copy of $P_5$ (see
    \Cref{fig:empty_component}). Thus, $V_{C-C_z}$ is empty.

        S'_{ab} \cup N_a \cup N_b \cup \bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}\setminus

This phenomenon happens because the exact neighborhoods of the induced copy of
the $2K_2$ have enough \textit{nice} properties so that they bypass every check
for $P_5$ in the algorithm. In particular, the induced copy of $P_5$ in
\Cref{fig:variant_chordal_1} arises from both $\mathcal{C}\setminus
\mathcal{C}_z$ and $C-C_z-I_C$ being nonempty for a $C\in\mathcal{C}_z$. Notice
that this relies heavily on the copy of $2K_2$ that the algorithm decides to
work on, since another choice for it could make the procedure detect a $P_5$.

In order to avoid this ``nondeterministic'' aspect of our algorithm, we would
like to recognize, even after finding a monopolar partition, if the graph is
$P_5$-free. Therefore, this section is devoted to provide additional checks for
the algorithm so that it can always return an induced copy of $P_5$ in case $G$
has one (when $G$ is chordal).

Observe that there could be a similar problem if, instead of $P_5$, $G$ had an
induced copy of the house, which in turn would imply that the original graph was
not chordal. However, we can circumvent this issue by checking, in linear time,
if the input graph is chordal. If that was not the case, then we can return an
induced cycle of length at least 4. If such a cycle has length at least 6, then
we can extract an induced copy of $P_5$ from it. Otherwise, if the cycle has
length 4 or 5, then the algorithm starts to calculate the exact neighborhoods of
such a cycle, as we will see in the next chapters. Although the algorithm can
skip it, the additional properties we are about to present do requiere to
account for the presence of cycles, since without it, the complete structure of
chordal monopolar $P_5$-free graphs would not be complete.

Before any results, recall that finding a monopolar partition of a graph is, in
general, a hard problem. For that reason, if the algorithm has already found a
monopolar partition, we would like to return such a partition, even when the
graph is not $P_5$-free. Thus, on a chordal graph, the output of the algorithm
will be either
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item a monopolar partition whenever $G$ is a monopolar chordal $P_5$-free
        graph, or
    \item an induced copy of $P_5$, possibly along with a monopolar partition,
    when $G$ is a monopolar chordal graph with an induced copy of $P_5$, or
    \item an induced copy of either $P_5$ or a minimal obstruction of
        monopolarity when $G$ is chordal but not monopolar.
\end{enumerate}

The next lemmas give us the properties we are looking for.

\begin{lemma}
    \label{lem:additionals}
    Let $G$ be a monopolar chordal graph with an induced copy of $2K_2$ having
    vertex set $Z=\l{a,b,c,d}$ and edge set $\l{ab,cd}$. Suppose $N_Z$ is
    nonempty and let $z\in N_Z$. Suppose without loss of generality that $N_{c}$
    and $N_{d}$ are empty. The following holds:
    \begin{lemenum}
        \item The set $N_\varnothing$ is anticomplete to at least one of
            $N_{ab}$ and $N_{cd}$. \label{lem:additional_2}
        \item At least one of $E_{ab}\cup E_{cd}$ and
            $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_z} V_{C-C_z}$ is empty.
            \label{lem:additional_3}
        \item Let $N_X$ be a bag anticomplete to edge $e$ of $G[Z]$ and let
            $N_Y$ be the bag complete to edge $e$. If $N_X\setminus N(z)$ is
            nonempty, then $N_\varnothing$ is anticomplete to $N_Y$. In
            particular, at least one of $N_a\cup N_b$ and $E_{cd}$ is empty.
            \label{lem:additional_4}
        \item At least one of $N_a\cup N_b$ and $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_z}
            V_{C-C_z}$ is empty. \label{lem:additional_1}
        \item At most one $C\in\mathcal{C}_z$ is such that $V_{C-C_z}$ is
            nonempty. \label{lem:additional_5}
        \item For $C\in\mathcal{C}_z$ each $C_z$ is a complete graph.
            \label{lem:additional_6}
    \end{lemenum}
\end{lemma}

We are ready for the final result of this chapter. Since we start assuming that
the graph $G$ is monopolar, we can use the fact that we have already provided
monopolar partitions, of which at least one is admisible for $G$. Hence, we will
follow the construction of the monopolar partitions given in the proof of
\Cref{thm:monopolar_chordal}, proceeding by cases to determine how sets interact
with each other and sorting out the cases that would yield an induced copy of
either $P_5$, $C_4$ or $C_5$. We refer to the proof of that theorem for the
names given to the sets.

\begin{theorem}
    Let $G$ be a connected graph. If $G$ is such that it has been assigned a
    monopolar partiton by the algorithm derived from \Cref{thm:monopolar_c5} and
    it satisfies every claim from \Cref{lem:additionals}, then $G$ is chordal
    and $P_5$-free.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    If $G$ is split, then we are done due to split graphs being
    $(2K_2,C_4,C_5)$-free, so they also are $P_5$-free. Thus, consider $G$ not
    split. Since it is chordal, it must have an induced copy of $2K_2$. Let
    $Z=\l{a,b,c,d}$ be a subset of $V$ such that $G[Z]$ is isomorphic to $2K_2$,
    and suppose $\l{ab,cd}$ is the edge set of $G[Z]$.
    
    Suppose that $G$ has been assigned the partial monopolar partition of Case 1
    before any subcases, that is,
    \[
        \p{N_a \cup N_b \cup \bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}
        \setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C \cup \l{z} , 
        K_{ab} \cup K_{cd} \cup \bigcup_{C\in \mathcal{C}_z} V_{C_z} \cup Z}.
    \]
    is a partial monopolar partition of $G$. By \Cref{lem:chordal_anti_3},
    $N_a\cup N_b$ is anticomplete to $K_{cd}$, while $N_a\cup N_b$ is
    anticomplete to $\bigcup_{C\in \mathcal{C}_z} V_{C_z}$ by
    \Cref{lem:chordal_anti_5}. So far, no restriction on how $N_a \cup N_b$
    interacts with $K_{ab}$ has been forced. For $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}
    \setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$, we know that it is anticomplete to
    $\bigcup_{C\in \mathcal{C}_z} V_{C_z}$ since they belong to different
    components of $G[N_\varnothing]$. Meanwhile, \Cref{lem:additional_2} implies
    that $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C} \setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$ is anticomplete
    to either $K_{ab}$ or $K_{cd}$. We proceed by cases:

    \paragraph{Case i: $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}\setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$ is
    not anticomplete to $K_{cd}$.} Then $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C} \setminus
    \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$ is anticomplete to $K_{ab}$. Moreover, $E_{cd}$ is
    nonempty, so \Cref{lem:additional_4} implies that $N_a\cup N_b$ is empty.
    Notice that no restriction on how $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C} \setminus
    \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$ interacts with $K_{cd}$ has been forced, only recall
    that each $V_C$ is not anticomplete to $K_{cd}$.

    The subsets of $V_G$ that remain to be assigned to either the independent
    part or the cluster part are $N_{ab}\setminus K_{ab}$, $N_{cd}\setminus
    K_{cd}$ and $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_z} V_{C-C_z}$. Nonetheless,
    \Cref{lem:additional_3} implies that $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_z} V_{C-C_z}$
    is empty, while \Cref{lem:additional_4} implies that $N_{ab}\setminus
    K_{ab}$ is empty. Thus, only remains $N_{cd}\setminus K_{cd}$.

    As exposed in the proof of \Cref{thm:monopolar_chordal}, $N_{cd}\setminus
    K_{cd}$ can be partitioned into two sets such that $S'_{cd}$ is stable and
    $K'_{cd}$ is either empty or a maximal nontrivial clique. To be more
    precise, let $I'_{cd}$ be the set of isolated vertices of $G[N_{cd}\setminus
    K_{cd}]$, and notice that $I'_{cd}$ is a subset of $S'_{cd}$. Then, from
    \Cref{lem:chordal_comp_2}, we have that $S'_{cd}\setminus I'_{cd}$ and
    $K'_{cd}$ are complete to $K_{cd}$. Finally, no restriction is imposed on
    how $I'_{cd}$ interacts with $K_{cd}$, while we know that each vertex in
    $S'_{cd}\setminus I'_{cd}$ is not anticomplete to $K'_{cd}$ by construction,
    and no further restriction is imposed. Thus, the monopolar partition of $G$
    is
    \[

        \setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C \cup \l{z} ,

    We claim that $G$ is chordal and $P_5$-free. Indeed, observe that the graph
    induced by all sets to the left of $z$ in \Cref{fig:chordal_structure_1} is
    a cluster, so they are $P_3$-free and, consequently, chordal and $P_5$-free.
    Thus, if we considered the induced graph generated by adding $z$ to the
    previous graph, observe that it has $z$ as a cut-vertex, so such a graph is
    chordal. Since $z$ is complete to both sets, it follows that the longest
    induced path in such a graph is of length $3$.
    
    Likewise, the graph induced by $z$ and all the sets (and vertices)
    to the right of it also induces a split graph. Therefore, those subgraphs
    are $(2K_2,C_4,C_5)$, so they are chordal and $P_5$-free. Hence, if $G$ had
    an induced cycle of length at least $4$, it would have to have vertices to
    the left and to the right of $z$. Nonetheless, $z$ is a cut-vertex of $G$,
    so no such cycles can be in $G$. We conclude that $G$ is chordal.  
    
    Similarly, if $G$ had an induced copy of $P_5$, $P$, such a copy must
    contain $z$ and it must have at least one vertex to the left and one vertex
    to the right of $z$. Since $z$ is complete to the sets to their left, it
    follows that $P$ has only one vertex to the left of $z$ and it is an end of
    path $P$. If the vertex of $P$ after $z$ is $c$, then the only way for $P$
    to be have $5$ vertices is with its fourth vertex in $K_cd$, which fails
    since $z$ is complete to such a vertex. The same happens for $d$, so the
    vertex of $P$ after $z$ must be in $K_{cd}$, let it be $u$.
    
    Since $I'_{cd}$ is stable, it follows that they neighbors of vertices in
    such a set are contained in the neighbors of $z$ and $u$, so $P$ cannot be
    increase to $5$ vertices and still be induced. Meanwhile, each vertex in the
    enclosed region not in $I'_{cd}$ is adjacent to $u$, so $P$ cannot have its
    fourth vertex in there. Therefore, the vertex of $P$ after $u$ must be in
    $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C} \setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$. Finally, as with
    $I'_{cd}$, we have that $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C} \setminus \mathcal{C}_z}
    V_C$ is stable and the neighbors of a vertex in such a set are adjacent to
    $u$. Thus, $G$ is $P_5$-free.

    \paragraph{Case ii: $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}\setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$
    is not anticomplete to $K_{ab}$.} Then $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}\setminus
    \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$ is anticomplete to $K_{cd}$. Similar to the previous
    case, \Cref{lem:additional_3} implies that $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_z}
    V_{C-C_z}$ is empty, while \Cref{lem:additional_4} implies that
    $N_{cd}\setminus K_{cd}$ is empty. Hence, we just need to decide how to
    partition $N_{ab}\setminus K_{ab}$, but do observe that in this case $N_a$
    and $N_b$ may be nonempty. However, \Cref{lem:chordal_anti_2_bag_2} implies
    that $N_a \cup N_b$ is anticomplete to $N_{ab} \setminus K_{ab}$, and we put
    no restriction on how $N_a \cup N_b$ interacts with $K_{ab}$. Thus, let
    $(S'_{ab},K'_{ab})$ be a split partition of $G[N_{ab}\setminus K_{ab}]$ with
    $S'_{ab}$ a stable set and $K'_{ab}$ is either empty or a maximal nontrivial
    clique, we call $I'_{ab}$ the set of isolated vertices of $G[N_{ab}\setminus
    K_{ab}]$. Once more, as stated in the last case, $S'_{ab}\setminus I'_{ab}$
    and $K'_{ab}$ are complete to $K_{ab}$, while no restriction is imposed on
    how $I'_{ab}$ interacts with $K_{ab}$, and each vertex in $S'_{cd}\setminus
    I'_{cd}$ is not anticomplete to $K'_{cd}$. Therefore, the monopolar
    partition of $G$ is
    \[
        \p{S'_{ab} \cup N_a \cup N_b \cup \bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}
        \setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C \cup \l{z} , 
        K_{ab} \cup K'_{ab} \cup K_{cd} \cup \bigcup_{C\in \mathcal{C}_z} 
        V_{C_z} \cup Z}.

    \paragraph{Case iii: $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}\setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$
    is empty.} As in the proof of \Cref{thm:monopolar_chordal}, suppose first
    that $N_{ab}$ is not complete to $z$, that is, $N_{ab}\setminus K_{ab}$ is
    nonempty. From that we deduced that both $N_{cd}\setminus K_{cd}$ and
    $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_z} V_{C-C_z}$ are empty. Additionally, we argued
    that $N_{ab}\setminus K_{ab}$ has a partition $(S'_{ab},K'_{ab})$ where
    $S'_{ab}$ is a stable set and $K'_{ab}$ is either empty or a maximal
    nontrivial clique. Notice that we end up with the same partition of Case ii,
    but now $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C} \setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$ is empty.

    Meanwhile, if $N_{cd}$ is not complete to $z$, that is, $N_{cd}\setminus
    K_{cd}$ is nonempty, we deduced that $N_{ab}\setminus K_{ab}$,
    $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_z} V_{C-C_z}$ and $N_a \cup N_b$ are empty.
    Additionally, we argued that $N_{cd}\setminus K_{cd}$ has a partition
    $(S'_{cd},K'_{cd})$ where $S'_{cd}$ is a stable set and $K'_{cd}$ is either
    empty or a maximal nontrivial clique. Notice that we end up with the same
    partition of Case i, but now $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C} \setminus
    \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$ is empty.

    Lastly, consider both $N_{ab}\setminus K_{ab}$ and $N_{cd}\setminus K_{cd}$
    to be empty, so the only set left to partition is
    $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_z} V_{C-C_z}$. If it is empty, then we are done
    and the monopolar partition is as in the one of Case ii, with $S'_{ab}$,
    $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C} \setminus \mathcal{C}_z} V_C$ and $K'_{ab}$ empty.
    Otherwise suppose $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_z} V_{C-C_z}$ is nonempty. By
    \Cref{lem:additional_1}, $N_a\cup N_b$ is empty, while
    \Cref{lem:additional_5} implies that $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_z} V_{C-C_z}$
    is the union of just one set, that is, there is a $D\in \mathcal{C}_z$ such
    that $\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_z} V_{C-C_z} = V_{D-D_z}$; this in turn
    implies that $V_{C_z}=V_C$ for all $C\in \mathcal{C}_z\setminus \l{D}$.
    Futhermore, \Cref{lem:additional_6} implies that $D_z$ is a complete graph.
    Finally, as in the proof of \Cref{thm:monopolar_chordal}, $V_{D-D_z}$ can be
    partitioned into $I_{D-D_z}$, the set of isolated vertices of $G[V_{D-D_z}]$
    which have no restriction on how they interact with $V_{D_z}$, and
    $V_{D-D_z}-I_{D-D_z}$ has a split partition $(S_{D-D_z},K_{D-D_z})$ such
    that $K_{D-D_z}$ is either empty or a nontrivial maximal clique which is
    complete to $D_z$. Notice that each vertex in $S_{D-D_z}$ is not
    anticomplete to $K_{D-D_z}$, so the final monopolar partition of $G$ is
    \[
        \p{S_{D-D_z} \cup I_{D-D_z} \cup \l{z}, K_{ab} \cup K_{cd} \cup

    chordal. Moreover, a path $P$ of maximum length would have its first vertex
    in the sets to the left of $z$, and its third vertex in $V_{D_z}$. Then,

    neighborhood of its third vertex, due to $I_{D-D_z}$ being stable.

    $N(x)\setminus \l{c}$ and $N(c)\setminus \l{x}$ induces a split graph.

    $c$ are cut-vertices of $G$, so it follows that $G$ is chordal. Lastly, a
    path of maximum length in $G$ has length at most 4, taking end vertices in
    the sets $N(x)\setminus \l{c}$ and $N(c)\setminus \l{x}$. Thus $G$ is
    $P_5$-free.

\end{proof}

We get the next result as an immediate corollary.

\begin{corollary}
    A connected chordal $P_5$-graph has one of the structures shown in
    \Crefrange{fig:chordal_structure_1}{fig:chordal_structure_4}.
\end{corollary}
